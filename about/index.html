About · who we are

About Lebob.

Lebob is a school robotics team from Perth, Western Australia. We compete in FIRST LEGO League as team 3236 and in FIRST Tech Challenge. In the 2025/26 Unearthed season we were Western Australian National Champions and competed at the 2026 Korea Open Invitational.

What we do

We build and program competition robots, and we run an engineering research project alongside the robot each season. Our current project is SoftSense, a soft gripper for recovering fragile artefacts from the seafloor.

We publish the work rather than keeping it. Every document from the 2025/26 season is readable in full on this site, alongside the original PDFs, and our code is public in the Lebob-Robotics GitHub organisation. As we put it in our robot design documentation, all our repositories are public, which means other FLL teams can explore our robot code, website projects and pathfinding experiments for inspiration.

How we work

The robot game plan for the Korea Open was built up to a maximum score of 545 points across seven runs, grouping missions by proximity so the robot completes several in one trip. The code is Python on PyBricks, structured with a drive base class, a mission control class that renders the hub menu, and missions registered through a decorator. The repository carries over 500 commits.

We learned to keep records the hard way. In our robot design journal we wrote that in the past we did not keep proper records of our testing or progress, and that this season we kept a real logbook, which gave us clear proof of what we tried, what worked and how we improved. That logbook is the run testing log published on this site.

SoftSense

SoftSense is our innovation project: a soft, sensing gripper that mounts onto an existing ROV manipulator arm so archaeologists can lift fragile artefacts off the seabed without crushing them. The fingers are passive Fin Ray trusses printed in TPU that wrap around whatever shape they meet, and the gripper reads its own grip force from the actuator's motor current rather than from any sensor at the fingertip.

It was reviewed by working subsea engineers, a practising maritime archaeologist and three Western Australian subsea operators, and it won a prize at the 2026 Korea Open. Read the full write up, or go straight to the engineering documentation.
