About · who we are

About Lebob.

Lebob is an independent, not-for-profit robotics team from Perth, Western Australia. We are not run by a school. We compete in FIRST Tech Challenge as team 29550 and in FIRST LEGO League as team 3236. In 2025 we won the FIRST LEGO League National Championship West, then competed at the 2026 Korea Open Invitational in South Korea.

What we do

We build and program competition robots, and we run an engineering research project alongside the robot each season. Our current build is our FIRST Tech Challenge BIOBUZZ robot for the 2026–27 season, as team 29550. Our most recent completed project is SoftSense, a soft gripper for recovering fragile artefacts from the seafloor, built during the 2025/26 FIRST LEGO League season.

We publish the work instead of sitting on it. Every document from the 2025/26 season is readable in full on this site, alongside the original PDFs, and our code sits in the public Lebob-Robotics GitHub organisation. Another FLL team can read our robot code, our website projects and our pathfinding experiments without asking us first.

How we are organised

Lebob is not a school team. Most of our members attend Perth Modern School, but the school does not run, own or fund us. The students started Lebob and run it under Lebob Robotics, an unincorporated not-for-profit association we set up ourselves, with its own ABN 41 535 175 584. We register for competitions in our own name, raise our own sponsorship, hold our own funds and make our own decisions.

Our members go to Perth Modern and no school stands behind the team. Descriptions of us as Perth Modern students describe where the eight of us go during the week. Lebob Robotics runs the team, and we run Lebob Robotics.

How we work

We built the Korea Open game plan up to a maximum of 545 points across seven runs, grouping missions by proximity so the robot clears several in one trip. The code is Python on PyBricks: a drive base class, a mission control class that renders the hub menu, and missions registered through a decorator. The repository carries over 500 commits.

Earlier seasons we kept no proper record of our testing, and our robot design journal says so. This season we kept a logbook, which gave us proof of what we tried and what changed as a result. That logbook is the run testing log published on this site.

SoftSense

SoftSense is our innovation project: a soft, sensing gripper that mounts onto an existing ROV manipulator arm so archaeologists can lift fragile artefacts off the seabed without crushing them. The fingers are passive Fin Ray trusses printed in TPU that wrap around whatever shape they meet, and the gripper reads its own grip force from the actuator's motor current rather than from any sensor at the fingertip.

Working subsea engineers, a practising maritime archaeologist and three Western Australian subsea operators reviewed it, and it won a prize at the 2026 Korea Open. Read the full write up, or go straight to the engineering documentation.

What is SoftSense?

SoftSense is Lebob's innovation project: a 3D printed soft gripper that bolts onto an existing ROV manipulator arm so archaeologists can lift fragile artefacts off the seabed without crushing them. It has no force sensor. It reads grip force from the actuator's own motor current instead.

How does SoftSense measure grip force without a sensor?

SoftSense reads the actuator's motor current, multiplies by the torque constant, and traces that back through the drivetrain to recover an estimated tip force. That removes every wire and connector from the fingertip, which is the part hardest to seal underwater, and the same channel also protects the printed gears.
